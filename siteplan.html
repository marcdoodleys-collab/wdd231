Site plan

This document lays out the subject, purpose, target-user scenarios, color scheme, typography, wireframes, and page structure that will guide the design and development of the Pet Haven project.

Identity

Site Name

Pet Haven — an online pet store and animal care center.

The name was chosen because it communicates safety, comfort, and belonging in two short words. "Haven" signals that this is a trustworthy place where an animal's wellbeing comes first, while "Pet" keeps the subject immediately clear to a first-time visitor. The name is also short, easy to say out loud, and easy to remember when a customer wants to recommend the site to a friend.

Optional domain

pethaven.org

Scope

Site Purpose

Pet Haven provides a single hub where visitors can browse pets currently available for adoption or purchase, read species-specific care guidance, and reach the store directly with adoption inquiries or general questions. Pet information is pulled dynamically from a local JSON data source so the catalog can grow without rebuilding the page, and the layout adapts responsively from a phone screen up to a desktop browser.

Target audience

Scenarios

Scenarios represent the questions a prospective pet owner — the site's core audience — would bring to Pet Haven. They shape what content the site needs to include.

  • ?What kind of dog breed would suit a small apartment with limited outdoor space?
  • ?How do I find out whether a specific pet is available for adoption or for purchase, and how do I contact Pet Haven about it?
  • ?What basic feeding and care does a new kitten need during its first few weeks at home?

Site structure

Number of Pages

Pet Haven will be built with 5 pages, which is sufficient to cover each scenario of the target audience without multiplying clicks or diluting the content:

  • 1Home — presentation, featured pets, quick access to other pages
  • 2Available Pets — dynamic catalog (JSON) of pets for adoption or sale, with filters
  • 3Care Guides — care tips by species (feeding, health, behavior)
  • 4Adoption / Contact — adoption request form and store contact information
  • 5Attributions — sources of images, data, and fonts used on the site
Why 5 instead of 4: a separate attributions page is generally required for the final project of the course, and separating « Available Pets » from « Adoption / Contact » avoids mixing content that is consulted (the catalog) with content that is filled out (the form).
